# Abbreviations

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/abbreviations

Abbreviations include acronyms, initialisms, shortened words, and contractions. The technical distinction between acronym vs. initialism rarely matters — "acronym" is fine for both.

- **Acronym** — formed from first letters, pronounced as a word: *NATO*, *scuba*.
- **Initialism** — formed from first letters, each letter pronounced separately: *CIA*, *FYI*, *PR*.
- **Shortened word** — part of a word/phrase, sometimes with a period: *Dr.*, *etc.*, *min*, *CA*.
- **Contractions** — see the separate Contractions page.

## Long and short versions of a word
Some words have long/short forms (*application*/*app*, *demonstration*/*demo*, *synchronize*/*sync*). Short versions aren't abbreviations — no period needed. Speaking test: if you'd say the short version as a word ("This is a demo version"), treat it as a word, not an abbreviation.

## When to use abbreviations
Abbreviations save time — but only if the reader doesn't have to stop and think about them.

- Use standard acronyms/initialisms that save the reader time.
- Spell out on first reference.
- Don't abbreviate terms unrelated to the document's main topic.
  - Recommended: "The internet of things (IoT) service can even be used for connecting to sensors in low Earth orbit."
  - Not recommended: "The IoT (internet of things) service can even be used for connecting to sensors in LEO (low Earth orbit)."
- Be wary of specialized abbreviations readers might not know (see Jargon).

## When to spell out a term
Spell out on first mention with the abbreviation in parentheses if likely unfamiliar: *Border Gateway Protocol (BGP)*. Use the abbreviation alone afterward. If used only once, include the abbreviation only if it's as commonly recognized as the spelled-out form.

- If first mention is in a heading/title, spell out in the first paragraph following it instead.
- Consider translation and reader familiarity — spelling out helps translators and readers unfamiliar with English, but skip it if it doesn't actually aid understanding (e.g., "portable document format" doesn't clarify what a PDF is).

### Abbreviations that rarely need spelling out
AI, API, DVD, file formats (PDF, XML), HTML, PC, RAM, REST, units of measurement (MB, MiB, GB, GiB), URL, USB.

### Format abbreviation introductions
- Italicize BOTH the spelled-out term and the abbreviation: "Establish *Border Gateway Protocol* (*BGP*) sessions..."
- Capitalize the spelled-out version only if it's a proper noun or conventionally capitalized — don't capitalize it just because the abbreviation is capitalized.
  - Recommended: "data manipulation language (DML)"
  - Not recommended: "Data Manipulation Language (DML)"
- Include the abbreviation in link text.

## Abbreviations not to use
- Never *i.e.* or *e.g.* — use "that is" / "for example."
- *etc.* is OK sometimes, but prefer different phrasing for most lists.
- Never internet slang (*tl;dr*, *ymmv*, *RTFM*) — write out the actual meaning.
- Use the most common form of a word — write *approximately*, not *approx.*
- Spell out shortened words/symbols substituting for words: "10 times faster," not "10x faster."

## Periods with abbreviations
- No periods on acronyms/initialisms.
- Period at the end of a shortened word — except date/time abbreviations.
- No period if the abbreviation is spoken as a word (*app*, *sync*).
- No period for country, US state, or DC abbreviations.

## Abbreviations as verbs
Never use acronyms/initialisms/shortened words as verbs.
- Recommended: "Use SSH to log in to your remote shell."
- Not recommended: "Then ssh into your remote shell."

## Indefinite articles before abbreviations
Choose *a*/*an* based on the pronunciation most common for your audience. Word-list preferences: "a SQL," "a FHIR," "an SAP."
