# Hyphens
Source: https://developers.google.com/style/hyphens

Use a hyphen when needed for clarity — to separate parts of words to avoid misreadings, or to combine terms that should read as a unit.

## General guidelines
Hyphenation depends on location (does the term precede a noun or follow a verb?), interpretation/readability (would omitting the hyphen create ambiguity?), and convention (some terms are always/never hyphenated regardless of other guidance). If unsure, check in this order: (1) the documentation you're working in, for an established convention; (2) this guide's word list; (3) Merriam-Webster. Deviate when it serves readers (see Break the rules).

Don't use a hyphen or a double hyphen in place of a dash. A dash is a distinct punctuation mark with its own uses (see Dashes).

## Prefixes
Generally, no hyphen between a prefix and the main noun: *infrastructure*, *megabyte*, *metadata*, *preprocessing*, *pseudocode*, *semiconductor*.

### Exceptions — hyphenate after a prefix when:
- The prefix is *self* or *cross*: *self-managing*, *cross-region*.
- The noun is capitalized or a number: *non-Google*, *post-2000*.
- Needed to avoid confusion/misreading: *de-energize*, *intra-index*, *re-mark*, *re-sign*.
- The base term already has hyphens/spaces: *un-Google-like*, *non-twentieth-century*.
- For consistency within a document: *pre-processing*, *post-processing*.

### The "non" prefix
Follows the same rules but often needs a hyphen because it easily forms hard-to-parse words. Use judgment; be consistent within a document.
- Recommended (closed): *noncurrent*, *nonempty*, *noninteractive*, *nonpublic*.
- Recommended (hyphenated): *non-existence*, *non-integer*, *non-key*, *non-managed*, *non-negative*.
- Add a hyphen before a hyphenated compound word: *non-KSA-based*, *non-self-sustaining*.

## Compounds
A compound combines more than one word: closed (one word, no space), open (space between words), or hyphenated.

### Compound nouns
Generally write compound nouns in closed form. If Merriam-Webster shows a two-word/hyphenated form but the closed form is the predominant or trending convention, use the closed form.
- Recommended: *webpage*, *hostname*, *tradeoff*, *workaround*.
- Word list has exceptions for well-established terms (e.g., *multi-region*, *style sheet*).
- Hyphenate multiplied units of measurement: "5 vCPU-hours," "40 person-hours."

### Compound modifiers before a noun
Hyphenate for clarity when a compound modifier precedes a noun — rarely wrong to do so.
- Recommended: "A well-designed app." / "Android-specific techniques."
- Use a hyphen after *more* or *most* if needed to clarify what they modify: "Edge locations with more-reliable internet links."
- Avoid compound modifiers with more than two words — move words after the noun instead; if unavoidable, hyphenate between each word as needed.
  - Recommended: "test cases that are specific to the 2023 edition" / "cross-data-center replication"
  - Not recommended: "edition-2023-specific test cases"

### Numbers and units of measurement
Hyphenate a number + spelled-out unit modifying a noun: "a 64-bit system," "100,000-byte files," "a five-minute wait." Don't hyphenate if the unit is abbreviated, unless needed for clarity — use a nonbreaking space instead: "200 GB disk," "50 Mbps connection." See Units of measurement.

### Adverbs ending in -ly
Never hyphenate: "Publicly available implementations," not "Publicly-available implementations."

### Conventionally unhyphenated compounds
Don't hyphenate compounds that convention doesn't hyphenate — check the word list or Merriam-Webster: "A managed instance group (MIG)." / "A machine learning model."

## Compound terms after a verb
Generally no hyphen needed after a verb: "The app is well designed." / "The logs are written in real time." / "The product supports high availability." / "Customers can use the utility as is."

### Exceptions — always hyphenated, even after a verb
Check the word list/Merriam-Webster: "You can deploy the app on-premises." / "...create an add-on." / "...apps that are cloud-based and cloud-adjacent." / "This page is customer-facing." / "...designed to be user-friendly." / "...an experience that's game-like."

## Range of numbers
Use a hyphen (not an en dash) for a number range: "8-20 files," "5-10 minutes." If ambiguous, use words instead ("from 8 to 20 files") — never mix hyphens with words ("from 8-20 files" is wrong). See Ranges of numbers with units.

## Spaces around hyphens
Never a space on either side of a hyphen — except a suspended hyphen, which can have a space AFTER (never before) it.
- Recommended: "You can set up the system to scan for new files at one- or two-hour intervals."
- Recommended: "...at one-, two-, or three-hour intervals."
