# Commas
Source: https://developers.google.com/style/commas

## Serial (Oxford) comma
Use a serial comma before the final "and"/"or" in a list of three or more items, to avoid ambiguity.

## Independent clauses
When a coordinating conjunction (*and*, *but*, *or*, *nor*, *for*, *so*, *yet*) joins two independent clauses, insert a comma before the conjunction — unless both clauses are very short.

## Independent and dependent clauses
When a coordinating conjunction separates an independent clause and a dependent clause, insert a comma only if the sentence could be misread without one. Otherwise, leave it out.

## Introductory words/phrases
Place a comma after an introductory word or phrase.

## Nonrestrictive clauses
Use a comma before *which* at the start of a nonrestrictive clause (see Pronouns for *that* vs. *which*).

## Conjunctive adverbs
Put a semicolon, a period, or a dash before a conjunctive adverb (such as *otherwise*, *however*, or *therefore*), and a comma after it, when it joins two independent clauses.

## Because
Generally avoid a comma before *because*, unless it starts a nonrestrictive clause.
