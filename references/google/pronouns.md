# Pronouns

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/pronouns

Ensure every pronoun clearly refers to its antecedent.

## Ambiguous pronoun references
Avoid vague pronoun-antecedent connections.
- Recommended: "If you type text in the field, the text doesn't change." — Not recommended: "...it doesn't change."
- Recommended: "The name of the function to execute in the given script. The name does not include parentheses or parameters." — Not recommended: "...It does not include..."
- Follow demonstrative pronouns (*this*, *these*) with a noun.
  - Recommended: "Set this value to true." — Not recommended: "Set this to true."
  - Recommended: "These approaches are your best options." — Not recommended: "These are your best options."

## Gender-neutral pronouns
Never use *he/him/his/she/her* as generic pronouns, and never use *he/she* or *(s)he*. Use singular *they* (long-established usage, including Jane Austen; adopted by the Washington Post in 2015). See CMOS 16th ed. §5.225, "Nine techniques for achieving gender neutrality," for more options.

## Optional pronouns
Use *that*/*which* to reduce ambiguity even when grammatically "optional."
- Recommended: "Right-click the link that you want to open." — Not recommended: "Right-click the link you want to open."
- Recommended: "You can use other option parameters, which are described in the following section." — Not recommended: "...described in the following section" (dangling).

## Personal pronouns
Avoid first person (*I*, *we*, *us*, *our*, *ours*) except:
- FAQ questions.
- A document with first-person authorial commentary.
- "We" referring to your organization, after naming it: "Example Pet Store recommends... We cannot guarantee..."
Prefer second person ("you") whenever possible — see Second person and first person.

## Relative pronouns: that, which, who, whose
- *That* introduces a restrictive clause, no preceding comma: "The echidna that has a long snout is furry" (describes one particular echidna).
- *Which* introduces a nonrestrictive clause, preceded by a comma: "The echidna, which has a long snout, is furry" (describes all echidnas, mentioning the trait in passing).
- Never substitute *that* for *which* or vice versa — they mean different things.
- Use *who* instead of *that* for a person; if unsure, *that* is generally acceptable.
- *Whose* is the possessive of both *who* and *which*, usable for people, animals, and things: "Examine the variables whose values are set at compile time."
