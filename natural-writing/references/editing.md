# Editing

Read when revising supplied prose.

## Identify what must survive

Establish the audience, purpose, requested length, and source draft from the
conversation. Use approved wording and the author's own additions as evidence
of voice. Ask only when a missing fact would change the meaning of the edit.

Before cutting, identify the facts and distinctions: actor, action, object,
scope, timing, outcome, attribution, and uncertainty. An operational condition
can matter more than a polished sentence. Preserve it when shortening.

## Find passages that add little

Look for repetition across the whole text, not only within each sentence:

- An introduction lists qualities that later examples already demonstrate.
- Several sentences repeat the same balanced list structure.
- A closing paragraph promises the same contribution the examples establish.
- Broad claims displace an explanation of an actual action or result.
- A smooth transition conceals an unclear relationship or a grammar error.

Explain the practical effect: redundant, vague, hard to follow, or unlike the
author's other writing. Do not label an ordinary phrase an AI tell without
comparative evidence for that claim.

## Make the smallest useful changes

Cut repetition before substituting synonyms. Replace abstraction with a
supplied example, not a newly invented detail. Keep plain wording that already
works. Let adjacent sentences differ in length when the ideas require it.

Apply plain-writing selectively:

| Guidance | Use in natural prose |
|---|---|
| Active voice | Make responsibility clear, especially personal versus team delivery. |
| Familiar words | Prefer the author's normal vocabulary; retain precise technical terms. |
| Contractions | Keep them when they suit the speaker and setting. |
| Sentence structure | Repair tense and clause relationships without changing chronology. |
| Short paragraphs | Give each paragraph one purpose; avoid choppy one-line fragments. |
| Descriptive links | Name a source clearly when research is part of the requested answer. |

Do not force instructions addressed to a reader into a first-person bio, or
make a personal email sound like product documentation. Avoid blanket bans on
serial commas, lists of three, or sophisticated words. Correct punctuation is
not an authorship defect.

## Check the revised account

Compare the complete revision against the latest source. Verify that each
retained claim has the same actor, meaning, and qualification. Confirm that
cuts remove repetition rather than a distinct fact. Read for awkward tense,
missing objects, and repeated endings. Preserve meaningful user edits even
when an earlier draft was smoother.

Return a revision when requested. For assessment-only requests, quote the
smallest relevant passages and explain the concern without rewriting all of
the text. Avoid adding a second unsolicited version.
