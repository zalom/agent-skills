# Application examples

Read when editing application answers or when a factual-preservation example
would clarify an edit. These examples are illustrative, not reusable claims
about another applicant.

## Keep the operational distinction

Source:

> At Acme, I built an ATS integration with an embedded inbox so recruiters
> could continue candidate conversations after the chatbot workflow ends,
> in the environment they already knew.

Possible edit:

> At Acme, I built an ATS integration with an embedded inbox. It let recruiters
> continue candidate conversations after the chatbot workflow ended, in the
> environment they already knew.

The handoff from the chatbot stays explicit. Removing it would make the prose
shorter while losing what the inbox does. Keep present tense instead if the
author is deliberately describing current behavior; tense consistency does
not establish whether the system still operates today.

## Repair the sentence without inventing scope

Source:

> I also worked directly with CTOs across three projects to agree what comes
> before implementation began.

Possible edit, if agreeing on next steps is the intended meaning:

> I also worked directly with CTOs across three projects to agree on next
> steps before implementation began.

Keep the three-project scope. Do not upgrade the work to owning strategy,
managing the CTOs, or delivering outcomes absent from the source. If the
intended object of agreement remains unclear, ask for it.

## Keep attributed praise

Source:

> The client described the onboarding plan as bold and ambitious and backed it.

Keep this if it faithfully reports feedback. It is attributed praise, not an
unsupported self-description. Do not turn it into an industry award or a
measured improvement in onboarding speed. Do not add quotation marks unless
the wording is confirmed as an exact quotation.

## Cut a redundant ending

Source, following concrete examples of collaboration and coaching:

> I'd bring that same approach: build relationships, make expectations clear,
> share context, and help people work through problems together.

Consider ending with the example when the paragraph adds no distinct point.
Do not require every application answer to finish with a promise. Keep a short
employer connection when it actually answers the question about motivation.
